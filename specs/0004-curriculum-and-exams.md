# 0004 — Curriculum (stages 1–3) and exams

Status: delivered (2026-11-01) — G5 (human review of 30 tasks per grade) pending · Depends on: 0001 · Unblocks: 0005

## 1. TL;DR
We define the school curriculum (preschool, grade 1, grade 2) — starting with **social skills training** (conversation: greeting, thanking, agreeing/disagreeing, giving an opinion, saying "I don't know") — as task generators whose answer is known to the program, with adjustable difficulty, and exams in three variants: known types, harder than in training, and new combinations. Proof: generator tests and a fixed, hashed exam pool.

## 2. Abstract
The student is to learn "like a human at school, step by step": no coding, with mathematics introduced slowly and always in the context of language. The MVP priority is a measurable improvement in reasoning on **new** tasks, so the truth must come from code (manifesto §3), and the exams must contain tasks outside the training distribution. The scope of stages 0–3 has been agreed. Children learn to talk with people before they learn to read and count, so the first subject of preschool is **social skills**: polite routines, agreeing and disagreeing, giving an opinion with a reason, and — most valuable — honestly saying "I don't know" instead of making things up.

## 3. Objective
**Goals — curriculum:**
| Stage | Skills (generators) |
|---|---|
| 1 — preschool | **social skills** (see below); questions about the world from a mini knowledge base (colours, animals and their sounds, body parts), opposites, bigger/smaller, categories ("Is a cat an animal?") |
| 2 — grade 1 | reading comprehension (story from a template → who/what/where question), counting objects up to 20, addition and subtraction within 10 |
| 3 — grade 2 | order of events, cause–effect, addition/subtraction up to 100, one-step word problems, comparisons and relations (2–3 elements) |

**Goals — social skills (preschool):**
| Skill | Example | Graded by |
|---|---|---|
| Polite routines — greet, thank, apologize, say goodbye | "Hello!" → "Hi! How are you?"; "Here is a cookie." → "Thank you!" | code (list of fitting phrases per situation) + teacher rubric |
| Agree / disagree | "The sky is green." → "No, the sky is blue." | **code** — truth from the mini knowledge base |
| Saying "I don't know" | a question whose answer is not in the conversation or the knowledge base → "I don't know." | **code** — the generator knows which questions are unanswerable |
| Opinion with a reason | "Do you like rain?" → "No, I don't like rain because I get wet." | teacher rubric (an opinion + a reason) |
| Asking for clarification | an ambiguous request → "What do you mean?" | teacher rubric |

All dialogue tasks use the chat format: `<|human|>` turn → `<|student|>` turn.

**Goals — exams (for every skill):**
- `id` — the same types and difficulty as in training;
- `ood_hard` — larger numbers / longer stories / more elements than in training;
- `ood_combo` — new combinations of known elements (e.g. counting inside a story).
- Transfer measurement: the result on the grade N+1 exams before its learning begins.

**Out of scope:** stage 4+, coding, encyclopaedic knowledge outside the mini knowledge base.

**Success criteria**
- Every skill has a generator with a difficulty parameter and a deterministic `seed`.
- Exam pools: 300 tasks per skill per variant, frozen and hashed.

## 5. Functional Domain Changes
**Task format:** `{skill, stage, difficulty, seed, context, question, answer, answer_type, steps[]}`; `steps` is the step-by-step reference solution generated by code (the seed of a reasoning trace).

**Rules:**
- A task is passed when the student's normalized answer (after the `<|answer|>` token) equals `answer` (numbers: value; text: lemma/synonym from the list of allowed ones).
- **Passing a grade:** ≥ 85% on `id` and ≥ 70% on `ood_hard` for every skill of the stage [`ood` thresholds TO BE VERIFIED after the baseline measurement].
- **Forgetting gate:** a drop of > 2 pp on the exam of any earlier grade → version rejected.
- Exam seeds come from a reserved range; the training generator refuses to use them.
- **Honesty rule:** every exam variant of every grade contains ~10% unanswerable questions; answering them with a made-up fact counts as an error, "I don't know" as correct. Answerable questions answered with "I don't know" count as errors too (no hiding behind it).
- Rubric-graded skills (opinion, clarification, part of polite routines) are graded by the assistant examiner (0005) and reported separately; the pass decision of a grade relies on code-graded skills only. Until 0005, the opinion skill is checked by code for its *form* only (a stance and "because …"), and clarification by a list of fitting phrases.
- **Grading by answer type:** yes/no — the first yes- or no-word; number — the first number after the last "=", otherwise the last number (digits or words); text — an accepted answer appears as whole words and no competitor does (naming every option fails); phrase — an accepted phrase appears; echoing three or more words of the question is not a number or text answer.
- **Pool size:** 300 tasks per skill × variant, fewer when the skill's task space is small (a fixed knowledge base): an `id` pool takes at most 30% of the space so lessons keep the rest; `ood` pools may take all of it. Every pool holds 10% unanswerable tasks, spread evenly.
- **Disjointness:** the training stream drops every task whose prompt hash is in `hashes.txt`, so a lesson never repeats an exam prompt; the same fact may still be taught in other words.
- The curriculum increases in difficulty within a grade (`difficulty` 1→5) according to the student's results.

**Resilience:** a change of a generator changes the pool hash → exams are versioned (`exam_set_version`), results are comparable only within a version.

## 6. Critical Path
1. Task format (incl. chat format) + grader → 2. preschool social skills → 3. preschool world knowledge → 4. grade 1 (reading, counting) → 5. grade 2 → 6. freezing the exam pools.

**Completion condition:** `cerbeto exam build` creates all pools; `cerbeto exam run --student <version>` returns a results table per skill and variant.

## 7. Control Gates
| # | Type | Condition | Verification | Blocks |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| G1 | Unit | Every generator: 1000 tasks, 100% of answers agree with an independent test solution | `pytest tests/curriculum/` | 0005 |
| G2 | Unit | Same seed → identical task | `pytest` | 0005 |
| G3 | Unit | Grader: table of 200 edge cases (spaces, letter case, numbers in words) | `pytest tests/test_grader.py` | 0005 |
| G4 | Integration | Disjointness: 0 shared hashes between the exam pool and 1M training tasks | `cerbeto exam check-leak` | 0005 |
| G5 | Manual | 30 tasks per grade read by a human — sensible for a child | review | 0005 |
| G6 | Unit | Every grade's exam pool contains 8–12% unanswerable questions; the grader scores "I don't know" correct only on those | `pytest tests/test_grader.py` | 0005 |
| N1 | Negative | The training generator throws an exception for an exam seed | test | always |
| N2 | Negative | A made-up answer to an unanswerable question is never graded as correct | `pytest tests/test_grader.py` | always |

## 8. Expected Outcome
- **Direct:** the school curriculum and an independent examiner in code.
- **Measurable:** results per skill × variant × student version.
- **Side effects:** none.
- **Rollback:** previous version of `curriculum.yaml` / the exam pool.

## 9. Additional Information
- **Rejected alternatives:** arithmetic/coding from the start (contradicts "step by step, like at school"); assessment by an LLM as the main examiner.
- **Technical debt:** the preschool mini knowledge base is written by hand — small scope.
- **Exam set (2026-11-01):** 10,830 tasks in 48 pools (16 skills × 3 variants), exam set `124d0260b61d8c188663f867`; each pool 10% unanswerable. Leak check (G4): 1,000,000 training tasks → 0 exam prompts in the training stream (without the filter 301,401 would collide — the preschool knowledge base is small).
- **Baseline — growing student after the nursery (`growing/v0032`, K = 4, greedy):** no stage passed. Accuracy `id` / `ood_hard` / `ood_combo`: preschool — routines 17/38/12%, agree/disagree 37/40/37%, facts 11/18/15%, opposites 7/11/11%, sizes 24/33/15%, categories 25/14/22%, clarify 8/10/5%, opinion (soft) 10/9/7%; grade 1 — reading 30/16/24%, counting 20/10/12%, arithmetic within 10 11/10/3%; grade 2 — order of events 5/5/47%, cause–effect 14/4/51%, arithmetic within 100 10/10/1%, word problems 15/10/3%, comparisons 26/10/25%. Honesty on unanswerable questions is high (70–90% in most skills) only because "I don't know" is a reflex: the student also says it to up to 60% of answerable questions (opposites, arithmetic), which counts as an error. Results: `runs/exams/growing/v0032.json`.
- **Smoke check (`cerbeto school smoke`, not the 0005 school):** direct lessons from the generators — no teacher, no sleep, the student learns the reference reply with its step-by-step solution — plus nursery replay, one stage at a time. Purpose: a cheap check that tasks, format, grader and a 30M student can learn at all before building 0005. First run (preschool, stopped by hand after 13 of 30 min): 28,032 lessons, lesson loss 0.01, replay loss ~1.3–1.5 (language kept); student `checkpoints/smoke/v0001`; its exam is still to be run.
- **Sources:** curriculum learning (Bengio et al., 2009); GSM8K as a model for word problems (Cobbe et al., 2021).
- **Open questions:** `ood` thresholds after the baseline measurement; small preschool `id` pools (social.clarify 12, social.opinion 10, social.routines 70, world.opposites 44, world.facts 64 tasks) make those results noisy — more knowledge-base entries and phrasings would help.
